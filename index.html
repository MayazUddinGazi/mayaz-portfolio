About

Cement chemistry first, computation second.

My doctoral work sits between the mixing bowl and the model. On the experimental side I cast and characterise mortars containing biochar, fly ash, and other supplementary cementitious materials, including systems mixed with seawater and sea-sand, and follow what actually changes: hydration kinetics, phase assemblage, pore refinement, transport, and strength development over time.

On the computational side I build predictive and optimisation frameworks — physics-informed learning, Bayesian mix optimisation, and image-based microstructure analysis — that are trained and constrained by that same laboratory evidence. The goal is not a higher R², it is a model whose behaviour a materials engineer can defend.

Before Miami I worked mainly on data-driven prediction of concrete properties and on environmental modelling in Bangladesh, which is where the machine learning half of this profile comes from.

Current research

Five threads running in parallel at SMARIN Lab.

Biochar as a supplementary cementitious material

Internal curing, pore refinement, and carbon storage in biochar-blended mortar, tracked through TGA, isothermal calorimetry, and strength testing.

Seawater and sea-sand binder systems

How chloride-rich mixing water and fly ash together reshape hydration products, resistivity, and long-term durability.

Physics-informed machine learning

Embedding hydration and maturity relationships into learning frameworks so predictions stay physically admissible outside the training envelope.

Bayesian mix design optimisation

Multi-objective optimisation of sustainable mixes, balancing strength, cost, and embodied carbon under experimental budget constraints.

Microstructure image analysis

Deep segmentation of SEM images to quantify phases, porosity, and interfacial transition zones without manual thresholding.

Digital twins for concrete quality control

Linking plant data streams to physics-based models for early prediction and correction of batch-to-batch variability.
